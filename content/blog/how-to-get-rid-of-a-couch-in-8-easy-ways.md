Figuring out how to get rid of a couch usually comes up at the worst moment, a few weeks before a move, when the new place is smaller or the old sofa simply isn't worth the truck space. The good news is that you have more options than dragging it to the curb. This guide walks through eight of them, from donating and selling to city trash pickup, hauling, recycling and upcycling, plus when it makes more sense to keep the couch and move it. For each one you'll see what it costs, how much effort it takes, and which kind of couch it suits.

## Key Takeaways

- A couch in good shape can go to a charity resale store, and many Habitat for Humanity ReStores offer free pickup of furniture.
- Selling works best with clean photos, measurements and a clear description of any wear.
- In Boston, the city lists furniture as an item to donate or put out with curbside trash.
- Most furniture in the U.S. ends up in landfills, so donating, selling or recycling keeps a usable couch out of the trash.
- If the couch is worth keeping, it can travel with the rest of your move or go into storage until you have room.

## Donate or Give Your Couch Away

If the couch is clean and in decent shape, someone else can use it, and giving it away is often the fastest way to clear it out.

### 1. Donate It to a Charity Resale Store

[Habitat for Humanity's ReStore donation page](https://www.habitat.org/restores/donate-goods) says many ReStores offer free pickup of large items, including furniture, and that sales proceeds support Habitat's home building. Other charities, shelters and local churches may take furniture too. Call ahead, since most only accept pieces without stains, tears or pet damage, and pickup slots can fill weeks out.

### 2. Give It Away for Free

Post the couch on a neighborhood app or a local buy-nothing group. Free furniture in good condition tends to get claimed within a day or two. Ask whoever takes it to bring a helper and a vehicle big enough to carry it, and agree on a pickup time before you post the address.

## Sell a Used Couch

Selling turns the couch into money toward your move or a replacement. Facebook Marketplace and OfferUp are the most common places to list furniture locally. A few habits help it sell:

- Clean it first, including the cushions and underneath.
- Take photos in daylight from several angles, and show any flaws up close.
- List the brand, material, age and exact measurements.
- Price it against similar listings nearby, and update the listing if it sits.

### 3. Meet Buyers Safely

Arrange pickup in daylight, have someone else at home, and confirm the payment method before the buyer arrives. Large furniture usually has to be picked up from your home, so keep the couch near the door and the rest of the house out of the way.

## Dispose of a Couch Through the City or a Hauler

When the couch is worn out, the goal is getting it out the door without breaking any rules.

### 4. Put It Out With City Trash

Check your city's bulky item rules before you drag it out. Boston's [special collection items page](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/special-collection-items) lists furniture as something to donate or put out with curbside trash, while mattresses have to be scheduled or taken by a private hauler. Other towns set their own rules, so check before you put it out.

### 5. Hire a Junk Hauler

A junk removal company carries the couch out and disposes of it for a fee, which helps if it's upstairs or too heavy for you. Better Life Moving doesn't offer junk removal, so this one is a job for a hauler.

### 6. Recycle the Parts

Couches are hard to recycle, which is why most end up in landfills. The [EPA's durable goods data](https://www.epa.gov/facts-and-figures-about-materials-waste-and-recycling/durable-goods-product-specific-data) shows that of 12.1 million tons of furniture and furnishings generated in 2018, about 9.68 million tons were landfilled. Some recycling centers take metal frames, springs or wood, so ask what yours accepts and whether the couch needs to come apart first.

## Keep the Couch: Upcycle, Move or Store It

Sometimes the best way to get rid of a couch problem is to keep the couch.

### 7. Upcycle It

A slipcover, new cushions or reupholstering can give a solid frame years more use. If the frame is sturdy and the style still works for you, that often costs less than a new sofa.

### 8. Move It or Store It

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where Can I Donate a Couch and Get It Picked Up for Free?

Many Habitat for Humanity ReStores offer free pickup of large furniture donations, and some other charities do too. Most only accept couches without stains, tears or pet damage, so describe the condition accurately when you call. Pickup slots can fill up weeks ahead, so book early if you're working toward a move date.

### Can I Put a Couch Out With the Trash in Boston?

Boston lists furniture as an item to donate or put out with curbside trash, while mattresses must be scheduled for pickup or taken by a private hauler. Check the city's current trash rules for your street and pickup day before you put a couch out, and try to donate or sell it first if it's still usable.

### Is It Worth Moving an Old Couch to a New Home?

It's worth moving if the frame is solid, it fits the new room and you'd pay more to replace it. Measure the new doorways and stairs first. A couch that won't fit through the door or is falling apart is cheaper to donate, sell or discard than to pay a crew to carry it twice.
