Storage options for moving in Boston matter more than most people expect, because Boston lease dates rarely line up. Your old lease ends on August 31, the new apartment opens on September 2, and a renovation or a delayed closing can stretch that gap to weeks. This guide compares the ways to bridge it: holding your things with your mover overnight or for a few weeks, renting self storage, using a portable container, or storing for the long term. It also covers what to check before you hand anything over, and how pricing works when the same company moves and stores your belongings.

## Key Takeaways

- The four main choices are a short hold with your mover, long-term storage with your mover, a self storage unit and a portable container.
- Storing with the company that moves you means one pickup, one delivery and one contract, with no second crew handling the same boxes.
- Self storage costs less in labor only if you have the time, the truck and the help to load it twice.
- Before storing anything, ask who can access the space, how long you can stay and how damage is covered.
- With Better Life Moving, storage is priced by the cubic feet your items take up.

## Which Storage Option Fits Your Boston Move?

Which one works depends on how long the gap is and how much of the work you want to do yourself.

### A Short Hold Between Two Moves

When the gap is a night, a weekend or a couple of weeks, the simplest plan is to have your mover keep everything. The crew loads at the old place, your things stay with the company, and delivery happens on the new move-in date. This is the setup most September renters in Allston, Brighton and Fenway end up needing.

### Long-Term Storage With Your Mover

Renovations, a sale that closes months out, a semester abroad or a downsizing decision all call for longer storage. Keeping it with the mover still saves a step, because the same crew that wraps the furniture brings it back.

### Self Storage and Portable Containers

A self storage unit or a container dropped in your driveway can cost less if you load and unload it yourself. The trade-off is time and labor, twice. On a narrow Somerville or South End street, a container may also need city approval to sit at the curb, and many buildings have no driveway at all.

## What to Check Before You Store Belongings

Boston's humid summers and freezing winters are hard on wood furniture, mattresses and electronics, so ask how the space handles temperature and moisture. Then go through the basics:

- Who can get into the space, and whether you can visit your items.
- Whether furniture is wrapped before it goes in, and with what.
- How long you can store and how the bill is calculated.
- How damage or loss is covered, in writing.

Coverage deserves its own question. For interstate shipments, the FMCSA explains that under the basic option, released value protection, [the mover is responsible for no more than 60 cents per pound per article](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/valuation-insurance). A light, expensive item is barely covered at that rate, so ask what other options exist before anything goes into storage.

For moves inside the state, Massachusetts requires that [household moving companies be licensed by the Department of Public Utilities](https://www.mass.gov/guides/moving-within-massachusetts), and the state guide explains how to check a mover's certificate number before you book.

Fragile and high-value pieces such as art, antiques and glass tabletops need more than a blanket. Our [white glove crews](/white-glove-movers/) pack those items separately before they are stored.

## Bridging the Gap Between Lease Dates in Boston

Boston's September 1 lease cycle produces the most storage requests of the year. Thousands of leases end and start within days of each other, and the timing almost never matches. If your move falls near that date, book the move and the storage together, early, and confirm the move-out and move-in times with both landlords.

Street space is the other half of the plan. The City of Boston sells a moving truck permit that [reserves two parking spaces for one day, from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m.](https://www.boston.gov/departments/parking-clerk/reserve-parking-spot-your-moving-truck), and online applications must go in at least 15 days ahead. You may need one at each address, on two different days, if storage splits your move in two.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Best Storage Option for a Short Gap Between Leases?

For a gap of a night to a few weeks, storing with your mover is usually the best option. The crew loads at your old address, holds everything, and delivers on your move-in date, so nobody has to rent a truck or a unit and load it twice. It also keeps one company responsible for your belongings.

### Is Self Storage Cheaper Than Storing With a Mover?

Self storage can cost less in labor if you have a vehicle, helpers and time to load and unload the unit yourself. Once you count two truck rentals, two days of lifting and the risk of damage, the gap often narrows. Compare the full cost of both plans for your specific dates before you decide.

### Why Does Climate Matter for Storage in Boston?

Boston swings from humid summers to freezing winters, and that cycle can warp wood, mildew fabric and damage electronics left in an uncontrolled space. Ask any storage provider how it handles temperature and moisture, how furniture is wrapped before it goes in, and whether you can check on your items during the storage period.

### Do I Need a Parking Permit If My Move Includes Storage?

You may need two. A storage plan often splits one move into a pickup day and a delivery day, and each Boston address may need its own truck permit. The city requires online applications at least 15 days ahead, so apply as soon as both dates are confirmed with your landlords and your mover.
