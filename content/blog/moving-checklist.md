Most moves go wrong in the last ten days, when the packing that was supposed to take a weekend collides with paperwork nobody started. A moving checklist fixes that by working backward from your move date, so the slow tasks start early and the final week is only confirmation. This one runs on an eight-week timeline, with notes for Boston, where street permits and the September 1 lease cycle add deadlines of their own. Follow it in order and you will always know what is done, what is next and what can wait until the weekend.

## Key Takeaways

- Book your mover four to eight weeks out, and earlier for any date near September 1 or the end of a month.
- In Boston, an online application for a moving truck permit has to go in at least 15 days before the move.
- Pack in reverse order of use: storage areas and off-season items first, the kitchen and daily essentials last.
- File your USPS change of address and schedule utility shut-off and start dates two to three weeks out.
- The final week is for confirming arrival times, building access and parking, not for starting new tasks.

## Your Moving Checklist: 8 to 4 Weeks Before the Move

The first month is for decisions, not boxes. Book the mover before anything else. Crews across Greater Boston fill up around the first of every month, and anything touching the September 1 lease cycle in Allston and Brighton books out well ahead.

- Get written quotes from more than one mover, and ask each one what the price includes. The FMCSA lists [an estimate given by phone or online, sight unseen](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/docs/Protect_Your_Move_Brochure.pdf) among the red flags of a problem mover.
- Ask your building manager whether movers must provide a certificate of insurance, and pass the request to your mover early.
- Sort every room into keep, sell, donate and toss, starting with the bulky furniture.
- Moving within Boston? Reserve street space. The city's [moving truck permit](https://www.boston.gov/departments/parking-clerk/reserve-parking-spot-your-moving-truck) holds two spaces from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m., and online applications must go in at least 15 days before your date.

Finish these four and the rest of the list runs downhill.

## 3 to 2 Weeks Out: The Packing Timeline

Packing runs in reverse order of use. The basement, the bookshelves and the winter coats can sit in boxes for weeks without anyone noticing. The coffee maker can't. If you would rather not spend two weekends taping cartons, our [packing crew](/packing-services/) can take a single room or the whole home, and labels everything by room.

- Pack storage areas, books, off-season clothing and decor first, then guest rooms, and the kitchen last.
- Label every box with its destination room and a one-line summary of what is inside.
- File your change of address through the Postal Service. [Standard mail forwarding runs for 12 months](https://www.usps.com/manage/forward.htm?kbid=93121), and extensions are available for a fee.
- Schedule the utility shut-off at the old address and the start dates at the new one, internet first.

Two focused weekends cover all of this for most apartments.

## Moving Week: Confirm Everything and Pack the Last Rooms

The final week is confirmation, not new work. Reconfirm the crew's arrival time, the elevator reservation if your building uses one, and where the truck will park. If your lease ends before the new one starts, don't solve the gap with a friend's basement. Ask about [storage between your two addresses](/moving-and-storage/) so one company covers the whole stretch.

- Confirm arrival time, building access and truck parking with your mover.
- Finish every room except one open-first box for the kitchen and one for the bathroom.
- Pack an essentials bag: documents, medication, chargers and first-night bedding.
- Empty and defrost the fridge and freezer a day or two ahead.
- Set aside keys, cash and small valuables to travel with you, not on the truck.

## Moving Day, Step by Step

On the day itself, your job is to answer questions and check rooms. The lifting belongs to the crew. Our crews for [local moves around Boston](/local-movers/) work in the same order on every job:

1. Walk the crew lead through the home and point out anything fragile, sold or staying behind.
2. The crew protects floors and doorways, wraps each piece before it leaves the room and straps it inside the truck.
3. While they load, do a final sweep of closets, cabinets, the medicine cabinet and the basement.
4. At the new address, call out rooms as boxes come in and let the crew put back together the furniture they took apart.
5. Check each room against your labels before the crew leaves, and keep every document from the move.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Soon Should I Start Packing for a Move?

Start packing two to three weeks before a typical Boston apartment move, and closer to four weeks for a full house. Begin with storage areas, books and off-season clothing, then work toward the kitchen, leaving the things you use every day for the final two or three days before the truck arrives.

If the calendar has already collapsed, a packing crew can take over a single room or the whole home.

### What Should I Include on My Moving Day Checklist?

A moving day checklist covers four things: confirmed building access and parking for the truck, an essentials bag that travels with you, a final sweep of closets, cabinets and the basement, and the paperwork for your move, including the written quote and any certificate of insurance your building asked for.

In Boston, add the posted permit signs so the reserved spaces are clear when the truck pulls up.

### How Far in Advance Should I Book Movers in Boston?

Book Boston movers two to six weeks ahead for most local dates, and earlier for the last few days of any month. The September 1 lease cycle in Allston and Brighton fills furthest in advance, so treat a September 1 date as a book-it-now item on your checklist, the same week you sign the new lease.

Short-notice moves are possible whenever availability allows, so a late start means a phone call, not a lost cause.
