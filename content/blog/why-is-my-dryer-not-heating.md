A dryer not heating is one of the most common calls we get, and the cause is usually one of five parts or a blocked vent. This guide walks you through each one in the order our technicians check them, shows you what you can safely look at yourself in a few minutes, and explains when the job needs a professional. By the end you will know whether you are dealing with a quick fix like a clogged lint path or a failed heating part that needs a replacement, and what to tell the technician when you call so the visit goes faster.

## Key Takeaways

- A blocked vent is the most common reason a dryer stops heating, and it is also a fire risk.
- Electric dryers usually lose heat through the element, thermal fuse or a thermostat.
- Gas dryers usually lose heat through the igniter or the gas valve coils.
- A blown thermal fuse is a symptom of poor airflow, so replacing it alone rarely lasts.
- Check both breakers on an electric dryer before you book a repair.

## The Most Common Reasons a Dryer Stops Heating

### A Clogged Vent or Lint Path

Restricted airflow makes the dryer overheat, which trips its safety parts and cuts the heat. Safety guidance from the National Fire Protection Association names [failure to clean dryers as the leading cause of dryer fires](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2016/06/f32/NFPA_DryerWasherSafetyTips.pdf) and recommends cleaning the vent at least once a year.

### A Blown Thermal Fuse

The thermal fuse cuts power to the heat source when the dryer runs too hot. If it blew, something made the dryer overheat, and the vent is the usual suspect.

### A Failed Heating Element

On electric dryers the element can burn out after years of long cycles. A continuity test confirms it in a few minutes.

### A Bad Igniter or Gas Valve Coils

On gas dryers the igniter or the valve coils are the usual cause. Because gas is involved, leave this one to a professional.

### A Tripped Breaker on One Leg

Electric dryers draw from two breakers. If only one trips, the drum can still turn while the heat stays off.

## What You Can Safely Check Yourself

1. Clean the lint screen, then run the dryer and feel for strong airflow at the outside vent.
2. Check both breakers for an electric dryer.
3. Make sure the outdoor vent flap opens while the dryer runs.

A U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission review of dryer fire investigations found the [duct and venting system was the most frequently reported fire origin](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/clothes_1.pdf), so a clear vent protects your home as well as your dryer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is It Safe to Keep Using a Dryer That Does Not Heat?

Running a dryer that spins without heat is not dangerous by itself, but the cause often is. If the dryer lost heat because it overheated, a blocked vent may be trapping hot air and lint, which is a real fire risk. Stop using it, clean the lint screen and check the vent before you run another load.

### How Much Does It Cost to Fix a Dryer That Is Not Heating?

The cost depends on which part failed. A thermal fuse or thermostat is one of the cheaper repairs, while a heating element, igniter or gas valve coils cost more. You get a written price after the technician finds the fault, and the service call fee is waived when you approve the repair.

### How Long Does a Dryer Heating Repair Take?

Most dryer heating repairs take about an hour once the technician is on site, because the common heat parts travel on the van. The technician tests the heat circuit and the airflow, replaces the failed part and then runs a full heat cycle to confirm the dryer is heating properly before leaving your home.

### Why Does My Dryer Keep Blowing the Thermal Fuse?

A thermal fuse blows when the dryer gets too hot, so a fuse that keeps failing points to an airflow problem. Long attic or roof vent runs, crushed ducts and lint buildup are the usual causes. Replacing the fuse without fixing the airflow means it will blow again, so we check and clear the vent too.
