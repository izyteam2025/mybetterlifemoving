Knowing what to do before moving day is mostly about the jobs that can't be done on the day itself: the fridge that needs a day to defrost, the elevator that has to be booked, the bed frame that needs an Allen key nobody can find. When those are handled in the days before, the crew walks in, loads and leaves, and you pay for moving rather than waiting. This guide covers the prep in three stages: what to hand your movers early, what to do in the last 72 hours, and the mistakes that most often slow a moving day down. It assumes your boxes are mostly packed already.

## Key Takeaways

- Give your mover the move date, both addresses, floor numbers, elevator details and a rough inventory as early as possible.
- Keep one moving folder, paper or digital, with the quote, the inventory, building rules and receipts.
- Defrost the freezer, empty dressers that go down stairs and take apart what you can in the 72 hours before the crew arrives.
- Book parking and elevators at both addresses, and put the confirmations in the folder.
- On hourly local moves, every minute the crew spends waiting for you to finish packing is on the bill.

## What to Share With Your Movers Before Moving Day

The more your mover knows ahead of time, the better the crew size, truck and timing will match your home. Send these as early as you can:

1. The move date and any flexibility around it.
2. Both addresses, the floors, and whether each building has an elevator.
3. A room-by-room list of furniture and roughly how many boxes.
4. Anything heavy, fragile or unusual: a piano, a safe, a glass tabletop, a large mirror.
5. Whether you need packing, storage, or both.

Keep all of it, plus the written quote and your building's moving rules, in one moving folder. When someone asks on the day what the building allows, the answer is in one place. Our [local moving crews](/local-movers/) use those details to decide how many movers and which truck to send.

## The Last 72 Hours Before the Truck Arrives

This is the stretch where small jobs pile up. Work through them in this order.

### Three Days Out

- Confirm the arrival time with your mover.
- Confirm the elevator reservation and loading rules at both buildings.
- Start using up frozen food, and plan the last two days of meals around what is left.
- Put screws and small hardware from anything you've taken apart into labelled bags.

### Two Days Out

- Defrost and dry the freezer, and leave the door open so it airs out.
- Empty dressers and nightstands that have to be carried down stairs.
- Take apart bed frames and tables you can manage. For pieces you can't, our [furniture moving service](/furniture-movers/) includes taking them apart and putting them back together.
- Disconnect electronics and photograph the cables before you unplug them.

### The Night Before

- Pack the bag that rides with you: documents, medication, chargers, a change of clothes, toiletries and first-night bedding.
- Set aside keys, cash and small valuables to carry yourself.
- Clear hallways, stairs and the path to the door.
- Strip the beds and pack the sheets in a box labelled "open first".

## Getting Your Building and Street Ready

In Boston, the truck needs a place to stand. The city's [moving truck permit](https://www.boston.gov/departments/parking-clerk/reserve-parking-spot-your-moving-truck) reserves two spaces from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m., and online applications need at least 15 days' notice, so this item belongs early in your planning even though it matters on the day.

Inside, ask the building manager about freight elevator hours, floor and door protection rules and any certificate of insurance requirement, and pass the request to your mover well before moving day.

Mail is the other loose end. Submit a change of address through the Postal Service; [standard forwarding lasts 12 months](https://www.usps.com/manage/forward.htm?kbid=93121), and you can pay to extend it.

## Mistakes That Slow Down Moving Day

Most delays come from the same few habits:

- Packing still in progress when the crew arrives. Massachusetts notes that the final cost of a local move depends on [the hourly rate, the packing completed and any added services](https://www.mass.gov/guides/moving-within-massachusetts), so unfinished packing costs money.
- No parking plan, which turns a short carry into a long one.
- Furniture full of clothes and books, too heavy to carry down a stairwell.
- Booking the mover in the last week of a busy month, when crews and dates are scarce.
- No one at the new address with keys when the truck arrives.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Should I Do the Night Before Moving Day?

The night before moving day, pack the bag that travels with you, set aside keys and valuables, strip the beds, clear the hallways and stairs, and confirm the crew's arrival time. Make sure the freezer is already defrosted and dry. Get some sleep; the morning goes better when you aren't packing at midnight.

### What Information Should I Give My Movers in Advance?

Give your movers the move date, both addresses, the floor numbers, elevator and parking details, a room-by-room furniture list, and any heavy or fragile items such as a piano or a glass tabletop. Mention whether you need packing or storage. Those details decide the crew size, the truck and how long the job takes.

### What Goes in a Moving Folder?

A moving folder holds the written quote, your inventory, the building's moving rules, parking permit and elevator confirmations, utility account numbers and receipts. Keep it in your bag, not on the truck. Anyone helping on the day can find the answer to a question without calling you, and nothing has to be looked up twice.

### Do I Need to Take Furniture Apart Myself Before Moving Day?

Take apart what you can do safely, like bed frames, table legs and shelving, and bag and label the hardware. Leave large or complicated pieces for the crew if your mover offers disassembly. Empty every drawer of furniture that has to go down stairs, because full dressers are heavy and can break.
