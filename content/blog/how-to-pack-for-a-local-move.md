Knowing how to pack for a local move saves more than broken plates. Even when the new place is three neighborhoods away, every box is still lifted, carried down a stairwell, loaded, driven, unloaded and carried up somewhere else. On an hourly local move, poorly packed boxes also cost time. This guide covers the supplies to gather, a two-to-three-week packing timeline, how to pack each kind of item, what to leave off the truck, and the extra steps that make a Boston walk-up or a small elevator building go smoothly. Use it whether you pack everything yourself or only part of the home.

## Key Takeaways

- Start packing two to three weeks before a local move, and earlier for a house with a basement or garage.
- Declutter before you buy boxes, so you aren't paying to pack and carry things you will give away.
- Put heavy items in small boxes and light items in large ones, and fill every gap so nothing shifts.
- Label each box with its destination room, a short list of contents and any handling notes.
- Carry documents, medication, cash, jewelry and anything irreplaceable yourself, not on the truck.

## Supplies and a Timeline for Packing a Local Move

Gather supplies before the first box is taped, so you aren't making runs to the store halfway through a room:

- Small, medium and large moving boxes, plus wardrobe boxes for hanging clothes.
- Packing tape and a tape gun, permanent markers and labels.
- Packing paper and bubble wrap for dishes and glass.
- Resealable bags for screws and small hardware.

Then work backward from moving day.

### Three Weeks Out

Declutter room by room into keep, donate, sell and discard. Pack seasonal decor, off-season clothes, books, guest room items and anything in storage.

### Two Weeks Out

Pack the living room, home office, dining room and closets, leaving out only what you use every day.

### One Week Out

Pack most of the kitchen, remaining clothes and household supplies. Keep a few plates, a pan and basic utensils.

### The Last Two Days

Pack daily items and make one "open first" box for the kitchen and one for the bathroom.

## How to Pack Room by Room

Finish one room before starting the next, and label each box as soon as it is sealed. Mixed boxes are what make unpacking take weeks.

### Match the Box Size to the Weight

- Small boxes: books, tools, canned food, files and small appliances.
- Medium boxes: kitchen items, toys, shoes, bathroom supplies.
- Large boxes: pillows, bedding, towels and lampshades.

A box should be light enough for one person to carry down a staircase. If the sides bulge or the top won't close flat, split it.

### Label for the Crew, Not Only for You

Write the destination room on the top and on one side, plus a short list of contents and "fragile" or "this side up" where needed. A color per room, with a matching sticker on each door at the new place, lets the crew drop boxes in the correct room without asking.

### Pack the Kitchen Last and Carefully

Wrap each glass and plate on its own. Stand plates on edge, not flat, and pad the bottom and top of each box. Our [packing crew](/packing-services/) often takes just the kitchen for people who pack the rest themselves.

## Packing Fragile Items, Furniture and Things Movers Won't Carry

Fragile pieces need wrapping on every side and a box with no empty space. Mirrors, framed art and glass tabletops are better packed by someone with proper materials, because a towel won't protect them on a truck.

Coverage is worth knowing before you decide what to pack yourself. For interstate moves, the FMCSA explains that basic released value protection [pays no more than 60 cents per pound per article](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/valuation-insurance), which covers little for a light, valuable item. Carry small valuables yourself.

For furniture:

- Empty every drawer of pieces that must go down stairs.
- Remove shelves and glass, and take apart bed frames and table legs where you can.
- Bag and label the hardware, and tape the bag to the piece it came from.

Our [furniture moving crew](/furniture-movers/) takes apart and reassembles larger pieces if you would rather not.

Leave flammable and hazardous items off your list entirely: gasoline, propane tanks, paint thinner, fireworks and open chemicals. Ask your mover for its list of items it won't carry, and plan to dispose of or transport those yourself. Perishable food, cash, jewelry, medication and important papers travel with you.

## Packing Tips for Boston Apartments and Walk-Ups

Boston apartment moves come with narrow stairs, older doorways, small elevators and very little parking. Before moving day:

- Measure doorways, landings and the elevator for your largest pieces.
- Reserve the elevator and ask about loading rules and hours.
- Keep stairways and hallways clear of packed boxes.
- Tell your mover which floor you are on at each end and whether there is an elevator.

How well you pack shows up on the bill. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### When Should I Start Packing for a Local Move?

Start packing two to three weeks before a local move, and earlier if you have a full house, a basement or a garage. Begin with seasonal and rarely used items, then pack room by room toward the kitchen and the things you use every day, which go into boxes in the final two days.

### How Should I Pack Heavy Items?

Put heavy items such as books, tools and canned food in small, sturdy boxes, with the heaviest pieces at the bottom. Fill gaps with paper so nothing shifts, and keep every box light enough for one person to carry safely down a flight of stairs. Rolling suitcases also work well for heavy, dense items.

### How Do I Pack Fragile Items for a Move?

Wrap each fragile item on its own, with padding on the bottom, sides and top of the box. Stand plates on edge, fill every gap so nothing moves, and mark the box fragile with an arrow showing which side is up. For mirrors, art and glass tabletops, consider having a packing crew handle them.

### Should I Take Apart Furniture Before the Movers Arrive?

Take apart what you can do safely, such as bed frames, table legs and shelves, and keep the hardware in labelled bags taped to each piece. Empty dressers that need to go down stairs. Leave complicated or heavy pieces for the crew if your mover offers disassembly and reassembly as part of the job.
