The difference between local and long distance moving shows up in three places: how the move is priced, who regulates it, and how the day is planned. A move from Brighton to Quincy and a move from Brighton to Philadelphia both need a truck and a crew, but the bill is built differently, the paperwork is different, and the timeline runs on a different clock. Knowing which kind of move you have tells you what to ask for in a quote and which agency to check the mover against. Here is how each one works, written from the crews at Better Life Moving, who run both from Brighton, Boston.

## Key Takeaways

- A local move stays within one state and is usually billed by the hour; an interstate move crosses a state line and is priced on what is moving and how far.
- With Better Life Moving, local moves are billed hourly with a 2-hour minimum, and long distance moves are quoted at a flat rate.
- In Massachusetts, local household movers are licensed by the Department of Public Utilities; interstate movers answer to the FMCSA.
- For interstate moves, a mover can't collect more than 110 percent of a non-binding quote at delivery.
- Local moves are planned around hours and street access; long distance moves are planned around an inventory and a delivery window.

## How Local and Long Distance Moves Are Defined

The line that matters is the state border, more than the mileage. A move from Boston to Worcester is a local, in-state move, even though it is a long drive. A move from Boston to Providence crosses into Rhode Island and becomes an interstate move, even though it is shorter.

That distinction decides which rules apply. Moves within Massachusetts fall under state oversight, and the state says [household moving companies must be licensed by the Department of Public Utilities](https://www.mass.gov/guides/moving-within-massachusetts). Moves across state lines fall under federal rules set by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration, and the mover needs a USDOT number. Better Life Moving is an FMCSA-registered carrier, USDOT 3866930, MC 1414489.

## How Each Type of Move Is Priced

### Local Moves: Hourly

Local moves are billed hourly, with travel time from our yard added. There is a 2-hour minimum that includes travel time, which keeps a small job affordable. Blankets, shrink wrap, tape and truck costs (gas, mileage, tolls) are included in the hourly rate. What changes the total is time: how much you own, the stairs, the elevator, and how far the truck parks from the door. Our [local moving service](/local-movers/) explains how crew size is set for different homes.

### Long Distance Moves: Flat Rate

Long distance moves are quoted at a flat rate, built from an inventory of what is going on the truck, plus the origin, destination and date. Traffic on the way doesn't change it, but adding a room of furniture after the quote can. ### How Interstate Estimates Work

Federal rules describe two kinds of interstate estimate. A binding one fixes the price for the listed items and services. A non-binding one is the mover's projection, and under federal regulations [the mover cannot require you to pay more than 110 percent of it at delivery](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/appendix-A_to_part_375), with any remainder billed after 30 days. Ask which kind you are getting, and get it in writing.

## Who Regulates Each Move and How Coverage Differs

Each kind of move has its own agency to check. For a local Massachusetts move, look up the mover's DPU certificate. For an interstate move, look up the USDOT number on the FMCSA website.

Coverage is a separate question from licensing. For interstate moves, the FMCSA explains that basic released value protection [holds the mover to no more than 60 cents per pound per article](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move/valuation-insurance), while full value protection makes the mover responsible for replacement value. Ask every mover which options it offers and what each costs.

## Planning Differences: Timing, Packing and Storage

A local move is planned around one day. The questions are street parking, elevators and how much is packed before the crew arrives. In Boston, the city's [moving truck permit](https://www.boston.gov/departments/parking-clerk/reserve-parking-spot-your-moving-truck) reserves two spaces from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. and needs an online application at least 15 days ahead.

A long distance move is planned around a window. Your belongings arrive after you do, so you plan travel and a place to sleep around the delivery date. Packing matters more, because boxes ride for hundreds of miles rather than a few blocks.

Storage works for both. When the dates don't meet, storage is priced by the cubic feet your items take up, and [moving and storage with one company](/moving-and-storage/) keeps one crew responsible from pickup to delivery.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Counts as a Local Move?

A local move generally stays within one state, such as Brighton to Newton or Boston to Worcester. In Massachusetts, household movers doing in-state work must be licensed by the Department of Public Utilities. Local moves are usually billed by the hour, with a minimum charge, rather than by weight or a flat price.

### What Counts as a Long Distance Move?

A long distance or interstate move crosses a state line, even when the drive is short. Boston to Providence and Boston to Raleigh are both interstate moves. The company needs FMCSA registration and a USDOT number, and the price is usually built from an inventory of what is moving, the distance and the date.

### Why Are Local Moves Billed by the Hour?

Local moves are billed by the hour because time is the main cost: loading, the drive across town and unloading. With Better Life Moving, travel time from our yard is added and a 2-hour minimum includes travel time. The truck costs and wrapping materials are already inside the hourly rate, so the hours are the bill.

### Is a Long Distance Move Always More Expensive Than a Local One?

Usually, because the truck and crew are committed for longer and the goods travel farther. But a large local move with difficult access can run many hours, while a small interstate move of a studio can cost less than people expect. Compare written quotes for your actual inventory rather than assuming.

### Which Agency Do I Check a Mover Against?

For a move within Massachusetts, check the mover's Department of Public Utilities certificate. For a move across state lines, check the company's USDOT number with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration. A company that does both kinds of moves should be able to give you both numbers without hesitation.
