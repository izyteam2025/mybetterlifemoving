What size moving truck do I need? Count bedrooms first. Rental companies generally steer a studio or one-bedroom toward a 10-foot truck, a two-bedroom apartment toward a 15-footer, and a three- or four-bedroom house toward a 20- or 26-foot truck. That part takes a minute. The harder question is whether renting is really the cheaper option once mileage, fuel, coverage, parking and your own weekend are added in. This guide covers how to size the truck, how to test the size against what you own, what a DIY rental really costs, and the moves where hiring a crew comes out ahead.

## Key Takeaways

- Size the truck by the rooms you're emptying, and check the rental company's own size chart before you book.
- Move up one size if you own a sectional, a king bed, a piano or a full garage, because rental ratings assume tight packing.
- A DIY move costs more than the daily rate: mileage, fuel, coverage, equipment and, in Boston, a $69 city permit to hold curb space.
- Hiring movers often costs less for walk-ups, large homes, out-of-state moves and moves with a storage gap.
- Renting a truck is usually cheaper for a small, short move with friends to help and parking by the door.

## How to Pick a Moving Truck Size for Your Home

Size the truck by the rooms you're emptying, not by square footage. As a rough guide:

| Truck Size | Usually Fits |
| --- | --- |
| Cargo van | A dorm room or a lightly furnished studio |
| 10-foot truck | A studio or one-bedroom apartment |
| 15-foot truck | A two-bedroom apartment |
| 20-foot truck | A two-bedroom house or a large two-bedroom apartment |
| 26-foot truck | A three- or four-bedroom house |

Each rental company publishes its own ratings, so confirm the size on its chart before you reserve. Those ratings assume a truck packed wall to wall with no gaps, which first-time loaders rarely manage. Between two sizes? Take the bigger one, because a second trip means another round of mileage, fuel and hours. Students moving out of a dorm can often skip the truck entirely and book [college moves](/college-movers/) sized for a room's worth of things.

## How to Check a Truck Size Against What You Own

Bedrooms are a starting point. Bulky furniture and the rooms nobody counts are what fill a truck. Go up one size if your list includes:

- A sectional sofa, a king bed or a dining table that seats eight
- A piano, a safe or home gym equipment
- A garage, basement or attic that's been filling up for years
- Patio furniture, a grill, bikes or kids' outdoor toys

List the big pieces room by room, closets included. That list is also what a moving company asks for. At Better Life Moving, a manager reviews your inventory and both addresses before quoting, so you aren't left at a rental counter guessing between 15 and 20 feet.

## What a DIY Moving Truck Rental Actually Costs

The daily rate is the smallest part of a DIY move. In-town rentals usually charge a base rate plus a per-mile fee, and the extras pile on from there:

- Mileage on every mile, including the drive back to the return location
- Fuel, since the truck goes back at the level you picked it up
- Damage coverage for the truck and for what's inside it
- A dolly, furniture pads and straps, often rented separately
- Parking, which is its own cost in a dense city
- Your time, and pizza for whoever you talked into helping

Parking is where Boston moves get expensive. The city's [moving truck parking permit](https://www.boston.gov/departments/parking-clerk/reserve-parking-spot-your-moving-truck) costs $69 for two non-metered spaces, online applications need a move date at least 15 days out, and the signs must go up 48 hours before the 7 a.m. start. Skip it and you may end up double-parked on a narrow street, carrying boxes farther than you planned.

## When Hiring Movers Costs Less Than Renting a Truck

Movers don't always win on price, but the math tips toward a crew in a few common cases.

### Your Move Needs a 20- or 26-Foot Truck

A big load is where DIY costs stack up. With [hourly local movers](/local-movers/), blankets, shrink wrap, tape and truck costs (gas, mileage, tolls) are included in the hourly rate. Local moves are billed hourly, with travel time from our yard added, and there is a 2-hour minimum that includes travel time.

### You're Moving Out of State

Long distance moves are quoted at a flat rate, built from your inventory and the distance, and our [interstate moving crews](/long-distance-movers/) work under USDOT 3866930, MC 1414489. Ask any interstate mover which kind of estimate you're getting. Under the [federal rules for interstate moves](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/appendix-A_to_part_375), if the estimate isn't guaranteed, the mover can't require more than 110 percent of it at delivery. Check coverage too: [FMCSA's liability rules](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/consumer-protection/protect-your-move/are-you-moving/liability-protection) say the basic level pays no more than 60 cents per pound per article.

### There's a Gap Between Move-Out and Move-In

With a rental, a five-week gap means loading everything twice, once into a storage unit and once back out. With [storage between your move-out and move-in](/moving-and-storage/), one crew picks up, holds your things and delivers on the day you get the keys. Storage is priced by the cubic feet your items take up.

### When Renting the Truck Is the Better Deal

A small apartment, a short distance, two strong friends and a parking spot by the door make DIY hard to beat. Timing matters either way: mid-week and mid-month dates are easier to book than weekends and the first of the month, and in Boston the September 1 lease cycle is one of the hardest times to find a truck or a crew.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Fits in a 10-Foot Moving Truck?

A 10-foot truck usually holds a studio or small one-bedroom apartment: a queen bed, a sofa, a small dining set, a dresser, a TV and the boxes from a small kitchen and closet. A sectional, a king bed or a full desk setup will usually push you up to a 15-foot truck. Check the rental company's chart.

### Should I Get a 15-Foot or 20-Foot Truck for a Two-Bedroom Apartment?

Most two-bedroom apartments fit in a 15-foot truck. Choose the 20-foot truck if you have a sectional, a king bed, a home office or a storage unit to empty as well. When you're unsure, size up, because a second trip costs more in mileage, fuel and time than the difference between the two rentals.

### What Size Truck Do I Need for a Three-Bedroom House?

A three-bedroom house usually needs a 26-foot truck, the largest most rental companies offer. A sparsely furnished three-bedroom apartment can sometimes fit in a 20-foot truck. Garages, basements and patio furniture push most houses to the largest size, and some need a second trip. It's also the hardest truck to park on a narrow street.

### Is It Cheaper to Rent a Truck or Hire Movers in Boston?

Renting is usually cheaper for a small move with parking by the door and friends to help. Hiring movers often costs less for a large home or a walk-up, because the crew's hourly rate already includes the truck, fuel, mileage, tolls and protective materials. Compare a full DIY total, not just the daily rate, against a written quote.
