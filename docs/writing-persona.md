## THE ONE RULE THAT OVERRIDES EVERYTHING

**A fact belongs to the market it came from. If nobody can show where a number came from, it doesn't get written.**

Better Life Moving runs crews in five metros from one Boston-founded company. The current site mixes them up: the Raleigh page uses a Charlotte photo, Boston alt text and a Waltham Thumbtack link, and claims "1,200+ Raleigh families rated us 4.9", which is the company-wide Google rating. Three things follow from this:

1. Moving is a high-trust, high-complaint category. One false local claim is enough to undo the rest of the page.
2. Google's quality systems look for near-duplicate city pages and claims nobody can check.
3. Each market has its own GBP. A city page has to match that market's GBP: same phone, same address, same name.

**On every sentence, ask:** is this true company-wide, or true for this market? Company-wide facts (USDOT, Google rating, founding year) can go on any page, stated as company-wide. Market facts (address, phone, crews, neighbourhoods) go only on that market's pages. A number with no source is cut.

## Brand Overview

Better Life Moving is a young interstate moving carrier, founded in 2022 by two movers, with its home base in Brighton, Boston. It runs its own crews and trucks for local, long-distance, office and specialty moves, plus packing and storage. It now operates in Raleigh, Charlotte, Chicago and Los Angeles as well. Boston is where the volume, reviews and calls are. The other four markets are newer and thinner.

On price it sits in the mid-market. Reviewers often say it was the cheapest of several quotes. Local moves are billed hourly with materials and truck costs included and a 2-hour minimum. Long-distance moves are flat rate.

The thing that sets it apart is people, not tenure. Reviewers name their crew leads (Nick, Vlad, Georgie, Daniel, Bek, Aziz) and ask for them again.

**Positioning statement:** A licensed carrier with its own crews, where the people who quote your move are the people who show up to do it, and the hourly rate already covers the materials and truck.

## Market Positioning

**Direct competitors**
- Boston: Boston Best Rate Movers (21 years, ~1,369 reviews), Safe Responsible Movers (Allston, next door), StairHopper, Brighton Moving & Storage, WellKnown Moving. Don't compete on years. Compete on review velocity, crews customers re-book, what the hourly rate includes, and Sept 1 lease-cycle readiness.
- Raleigh: Athens Moving Experts (~960 reviews), Little Guys, Bellhop, Undergrads Movers, WellKnown. Position as the interstate-capable carrier for people moving into and out of the Triangle.
- Charlotte: Gentle Giant, You Move Me, Flex, Easy Movers.
- Chicago and LA: crowded markets full of established local brands. BLM's review base there is thin. Write honestly and keep the scope small.

**Aspirational:** Gentle Giant (since 1981, multi-state). Not reachable on tenure. Its value is as a model of multi-market structure.

**Market challenges:** moving scams make readers suspicious of every claim. Large lead-gen sites outrank small carriers. Seasonality is sharp (August and September peak, a big drop in October). GBP views fell about 80% from August to October.

## Target Audience

### Primary: apartment and condo movers in Boston and Brookline/Cambridge/Allston-Brighton
Renters aged 22–40 and young families, many on the September 1 lease cycle. Students and recent graduates. Price-aware, mobile, booking on short notice.
- **Value:** a real quote, crews who turn up on time, careful handling on walk-ups and tight stairs.
- **Fear (in order):** the price growing on move day, damage, no-shows and unanswered phones, a crew that rushes.
- **How they decide:** Google reviews, then two or three quotes, then whoever answers fastest.
- **Not looking for:** luxury language, corporate relocation packages.

### Secondary
- **Long-distance households** leaving Boston for the Southeast (reviews mention Boston to Orlando and Boston to Philadelphia). These are high-value jobs.
- **Triangle and Charlotte relocators**, including new-construction and gated-community moves. The numbers on the current Raleigh page are unverified.
- **Small offices and retail** (office and commercial page).
- **Specialty items:** pianos, antiques, art (white glove; the old URL has backlinks worth recovering).
- **Military families** (PCS) and **students** (college moving).

## Brand Personality

If Better Life Moving were a person, they'd be the crew lead who texts you the night before, shows up early, jokes while lifting the couch down three flights, and tells you straight what the stairs will add to the bill.

| Trait | What it means for writing |
|---|---|
| Straight-talking | Say how billing works in the first answer. No "pricing varies". |
| Hands-on | Write from the moving day: blankets, shrink wrap, dollies, walk-ups, elevator bookings. |
| Upbeat | Light warmth is fine. Reviews praise "funny, friendly" crews. Exclamation marks are not. |
| Young and ambitious | Own the 2022 founding. Don't hide it or pad it. |
| Accountable | Name what can go wrong and what the company does about it. |

**Rules out:** corporate polish, fake heritage, scare tactics about "rogue movers", hype.

## Tone & Style

**Overall:** candid, specific, practical, warm, calm.

| Element | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Lead with the mechanism | Explain how things work before selling | ❌ "Transparent pricing you can trust." ✅ "Local moves are billed by the hour, with travel time from our yard. Blankets, shrink wrap and the truck are already in the rate." |
| Concrete over adjectives | Replace every adjective with a detail | ❌ "Our professional movers take great care." ✅ "Every dresser leaves wrapped in blankets and shrink wrap, and drawers are taped shut." |
| Local and true | Local detail only where sourced | ❌ "We know every Raleigh neighborhood." ✅ "Moving into a North Hills building with a loading dock? Tell us the reservation window when you book." |
| Honest limits | Say what depends on availability | ❌ "Same-day moves guaranteed." ✅ "Call before noon and we'll tell you whether a crew is free today." |
| Reader first | Use "you", keep "we" for actions | ❌ "We pride ourselves on communication." ✅ "You'll get the crew's arrival window the day before." (only once confirmed) |

**Example tone statement:** "You'll know how the bill works before we arrive. Local moves are hourly, with a two-hour minimum that includes travel. The blankets, wrap and truck are already in that rate."

## Voice Do's & Don'ts

### Do
1. Name the authority on trust sections: "FMCSA-registered carrier, USDOT 3866930, MC 1414489", linked to the public record.
2. Explain the hourly billing, travel time and 2-hour minimum in plain words wherever price comes up.
3. Write from the moving day: stairs, elevators, parking permits, Sept 1 in Boston, HOA gate codes in the Triangle.
4. Keep company-wide numbers labelled as company-wide ("4.9 on Google across 563 reviews"), and show them only through the widget or the stats block.
5. Use contractions, "you", and short sentences in process sections.
6. Mix sentence lengths. Open some paragraphs with the answer, some with a detail.
7. Answer the cost question with how pricing works, even when we can't give a dollar amount.
8. Give each market its own hero idea, FAQ set and closing.
9. Say "own crews and trucks", because that is what makes it a carrier.
10. Keep one CTA per section, and make the phone easy to reach (the main complaint is being hard to reach).

### Don't
1. No em dashes anywhere.
2. No "premier", "top-rated", "#1", "best-in-class", "exceptional service", "dedicated professionals", "stress-free" as a promise, "look no further", "whether you're…".
3. Never repeat the unverified Raleigh stats (1,200+ moves, 50+ new construction, 35+ families, "Raleigh families rated us 4.9").
4. Never write "as seen on NBC/Fox/ABC".
5. Never claim years of experience beyond 2022, or "family-owned".
6. Never say "flat rate" about local moves.
7. Never promise same-day or last-minute availability as guaranteed ("whenever availability allows" is the client's own limit).
8. Never paste review text or star counts as static copy. Use the live widget.
9. Never put another market's photo, alt text, phone or review link on a city page.
10. Never claim a state licence, membership or insurance amount we can't number.
11. No recycled FAQ answers across city pages, and no "[City] movers you can trust" headings repeated across cities.
12. Don't scare-sell ("avoid rogue movers"). Explain how to check a USDOT number instead.

## Messaging Priorities

1. **Carrier with its own crews and trucks.** Answers the scam fear. For the homepage, About, service pages and every city page's trust block.
2. **You know how the bill works before move day.** Hourly plus travel, 2-hour minimum, materials and truck included; flat-rate long distance. For service pages, FAQ and cost guides.
3. **Crews customers ask for by name.** Proof that the experience is people-led. Use crew names only once approved, otherwise speak about crews in general. For the homepage, About and city pages.
4. **One company from packing to storage to delivery.** For packing, storage and long-distance pages.
5. **Built for real moving days in each market.** Boston Sept 1, walk-ups and permits; Triangle new construction and HOA rules. Only where sourced. For city pages.
6. **Easy to reach.** Answers the main complaint. One clear phone number per market, quick quote form. For every page.

Blocked until confirmed: valuation options, guarantees, deposit and cancellation terms, languages spoken.

## Sales Process

1. Quote request by form (move date, size, origin, destination) or by phone. Live form fields: move date, moving size, name, email, phone, phone type, origin, destination.
2. A manager reviews the details and explains booking terms and any deposit (FAQ).
3. Local moves: hourly quote. Long distance: flat-rate quote.
4. Moving day: the crew wraps, loads, transports, unloads and places furniture.
5. Storage, if needed: pickup, storage, delivery on the chosen date.

Response time, confirmation steps, arrival windows and payment timing are not established. Don't describe them.

## Content Strategy & Page Types

- **Service pages:** answer the intent in the first two lines, then how it's billed, what's included, process from the moving day, FAQ unique to the service, CTA. Recover the white glove and last-minute URLs (redirects).
- **City pages:** a separate hero, proof and FAQ for each city. Market NAP block matching its GBP. Local logistics only where sourced (permits, building rules), logged in `docs/local-detail.md`.
- **Blog:** moving cost guides with third-party averages, clearly attributed (never BLM's price); Boston lease-cycle guides; city-vs-city relocation guides (the Durham vs Raleigh format already exists); packing and box-count guides (already live).
- **AEO:** put the direct answer in the first sentence under each H2 and each FAQ, as a standalone sentence a chatbot can quote ("Local moves with Better Life Moving are billed hourly with a two-hour minimum that includes travel time.").

## CTA Details

- **Primary:** "Get a free quote" → quote form (SmartMoving or GHL, CONFIRM).
- **Phone:** the market's own number, shown as text and as a tel: link. Never show two numbers on one page.
- **Form headline:** "Get your moving quote". Subheadline: "Move date, size and both addresses is all we need to price it."
- One primary CTA per section. Keep CTAs calm: no "Act now", no countdowns. Offers appear only with real dates.

## Blog Post Structure Guide

Follow the `blog-content` skill. Title Case headings. Headline formats in use: "How Many Boxes Do I Need for Moving? A Room-by-Room Count". Open with the answer. Costs get a named third-party source. FAQ answers are standalone sentences. Close with the quote CTA and the market phone.
